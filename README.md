# Ledger Eyes

Ledger Eyes reads what the crypto crowd writes and turns it into one small, fixed-format result file, the **brief** (`brief.json`). The brief is the interface: other programs read it, not the notebook.

The work is a single Jupyter notebook that runs from top to bottom on Google Colab. It uses the public StockTwits crypto dataset from Hugging Face, for its dated chapters two public dated datasets from Hugging Face (crypto tweets and crypto news), and, to set the daily results against the market, the public BTC daily closes of Yahoo Finance, Coinbase or Stooq and the Crypto Fear and Greed Index of alternative.me; it needs no keys or accounts.

Chapters so far: 0 The question and the data · 1 Loading · 2 A first look · 3 Cleaning · 4 Tokenization · 5 Training, validation and test sets · 6 Vectorization · 7 Models · 8 Evaluation · 9 Topics · 10 Why · 11 Emotions · 12 Dated data · 13 Day by day · 14 Two layers · 15 Against the market · 16 The brief: format, check, save and panel. Chapters 4 to 8 tell bullish from bearish posts and fill the brief's `sentiment` section; chapter 9 sorts posts into topics and fills the `topics` section, then puts the same posts into drawers, broad subjects chosen by a person through a versioned word list, and groups the posts in no drawer as a hint for new drawers (the `drawers` section); chapter 10 crosses the two (which topics and which drawers lean bullish, which lean bearish), calibrates the model's bullish share with its recognition rates from chapter 8, and fills the `why` section. Chapter 11 learns eight crypto emotions (and `none`) from silver labels, pre-labels given by a language model, and gives every topic post an emotion, overall and per drawer (the `emotions` section). Chapter 12 reads two public datasets with dates, tweets as the retail layer and news headlines as the media layer, into one dated post table and counts its posts per layer and UTC day (the `dated_data` section). Chapter 13 cleans and scores every dated post like the topic posts (bullish share at 0.5 and calibrated, emotion, drawers) and gives, per layer and UTC day, the bullish share, the emotions, the drawers and the bullish share in every drawer (the `daily` section, with example posts of a few chosen days), and the words that suddenly appear in many more posts than on the days before (the `new_words` section). Chapter 14 puts the media and the retail layer side by side on the days both cover and lists the days they lean opposite ways, and measures the sentiment model on the news headlines that carry a reader label (the `layers` section). Chapter 15 sets every layer's daily bullish share against the BTC price (the next day's change) and the Fear and Greed Index as Spearman rank correlations, describing the past only (the `validation` section). Chapter 16 checks the brief against its fixed format (see [docs/brief-format.md](docs/brief-format.md)), saves it, and shows its main tables on a small panel that reads only the saved file.

Besides the notebook, `scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh` gives StockTwits posts one of eight crypto emotions (or `none`) through a language model running on this machine, as training labels for chapter 11 (see [Emotion pre-labels](#emotion-pre-labels)).

## Run on Google Colab

1. Open <https://colab.research.google.com>, choose *File → Upload notebook* and pick `notebook/ledger_eyes.ipynb`.
2. Optionally change the switches in the first code cell.
3. Choose *Runtime → Run all*.

The first code cell after the switches installs the packages Colab lacks (for example `emoji` and `contractions`, and `gradio` for the panel when Colab does not have it); everything else, including TensorFlow/Keras and Hugging Face `tokenizers`, is preinstalled on Colab, and a package that is already there is left as it is. The same cell sets `GRADIO_ANALYTICS_ENABLED=False` before anything is imported, so Gradio sends no usage statistics and makes no version checks. The data is read straight from Hugging Face (`hf://datasets/ElKulako/stocktwits-crypto/st-data-full.xlsx`), and the brief is saved to `data/brief.json` inside the Colab session. Chapter 11 reads the emotion pre-labels from this repository on GitHub (`EMOTION_LABELS_SOURCE`, the raw address of `labels/emotions-prelabel-v1.csv`); that address works only once the file has been pushed to GitHub. Until then, upload `labels/emotions-prelabel-v1.csv` to the Colab session and set `EMOTION_LABELS_SOURCE` to its path; otherwise the notebook stops in chapter 11 and says so. Chapter 12 reads the two dated datasets from Hugging Face at pinned revisions (`DATED_TWEETS_SOURCE`, `DATED_NEWS_SOURCE`), without any token: the news are 12 MB, the tweets 14 Parquet files of about 256 MB each (3.6 GB), read one file at a time and three columns only, so the first run of chapter 12 takes a while; its result is cached in `WORK_DIR/cache/`. Chapter 15 reads the BTC daily closes from Yahoo Finance through `yfinance` (preinstalled on Colab, possibly in another version than the one pinned here), or else from Coinbase or Stooq, and the Fear and Greed Index from alternative.me, all without a key; whether Colab can reach them has not been tried. When none of the price sources answers, the notebook says so and goes on without the price.

The last cell starts the panel (`LAUNCH_PANEL = True`, the default): a small Gradio page with four tabs (what the brief covers, the evaluation table, the topic table, the topic × bullish table), read from the saved `brief.json`. It never asks for a public link (`share=False`) and listens only on the notebook's own machine (`127.0.0.1`); Gradio shows it below the cell.

The notebook has not yet been run on Colab itself (so neither has the panel); it has been run locally with the same code path, including reading the `hf://` address directly.

## Run locally

Requirements: Python 3.12 and a network connection for the first setup (package index, Hugging Face, NLTK data) and for the first full check (the price sources and alternative.me, see chapter 15).

```bash
# 1. Environment
python3.12 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt

# 2. Full datasets into data/raw/ (only needed for the full check)
scripts/download_data.sh            # StockTwits, 69.6 MB
scripts/download_data.sh --dated    # the two dated datasets, 3.6 GB, into data/raw/dlt-tweets/ and data/raw/dlt-news/

# 3. Checks
scripts/check.sh quick
scripts/check.sh full
```

The scripts use `.venv/bin/python` unless `EYES_PYTHON` points to another interpreter.

### Changing switches without editing the notebook

Both checks accept `-p NAME VALUE` (repeatable). The value is read as a Python literal when possible, otherwise as text, and applies to every run of that check (except `LAUNCH_PANEL`, which the checks always set to `False`):

```bash

scripts/check.sh full -p DATA_SOURCE hf://datasets/ElKulako/stocktwits-crypto/st-data-full.xlsx -p WORK_DIR data/hf-run
scripts/check.sh quick -p RANDOM_SEED 7
```

The checks pass the values to the notebook with [papermill](https://papermill.readthedocs.io), which replaces the cell tagged `parameters`. Without the scripts:

```bash
.venv/bin/papermill notebook/ledger_eyes.ipynb data/out.ipynb -p N_CLASSES 3 -p BTC_ONLY True
```

**Working sample.** Keep the classes in use (`N_CLASSES = 2` drops neutral), keep only Bitcoin posts when `BTC_ONLY`, then draw the same number of posts from every class with `RANDOM_SEED`: the smaller of `SAMPLE_PER_CLASS` and the size of the smallest remaining class. Chapter 2's statistics use `df_all`; chapter 3 cleans `df` only.

**Cleaning.** Lower case → extra whitespace → HTML tags → emoji to words (only with `EMOJI_TO_WORDS`; skin-tone qualifiers dropped, underscores turned into spaces) → special characters (keep letters, digits, whitespace) → contractions (`contractions` package; it also expands apostrophe-free forms such as "dont" and "cant", so negative contractions come back as "not") → stop words (NLTK English list, minus the negation words when `KEEP_NEGATIONS`) → WordNet lemmatisation.

**Sentiment models.** Chapter 4 only shows two tokenizers (NLTK words and a BPE tokenizer trained on the original text of the working sample); nothing from it reaches a model. Chapter 5 splits `df` 80/20 and then the 20% part half and half, both times stratified by label with `RANDOM_SEED`; every class needs at least 10 posts in the working sample, otherwise the notebook stops with a clear message. Chapter 6 fits a `CountVectorizer` and a `TfidfVectorizer` (`max_features=VOCAB_SIZE`, 32-bit floats) on the training set's `clean_text` and only transforms the validation and test sets; the matrices stay sparse instead of `.toarray()`. Chapter 7 trains one Keras network per vector type: a single `Dense` layer, with one sigmoid unit and binary cross-entropy for two classes (inside the network bearish → 0, bullish → 1) or three softmax units and sparse categorical cross-entropy for three classes (labels unchanged), Adam, `EPOCHS` and `BATCH_SIZE`, after `tf.keras.utils.set_random_seed(RANDOM_SEED)`. The validation set is watched during training and then used once to choose the model with the higher macro-F1 (a tie goes to `count_network`). Chapter 8 is the only place that evaluates on the test set; chapter 10 later scores the test posts that are also in the topic post table, only to count them, and nothing is trained or chosen after that.

**Topics.** Chapter 9 builds `topic_posts` from `df_all`: with `TOPIC_SCOPE = "btc"` every post with `mentions_btc`, with `"all"` every post; when more than `TOPIC_MAX_POSTS` posts are in scope, `DataFrame.sample(n=TOPIC_MAX_POSTS, random_state=RANDOM_SEED)` keeps that many, put back into file order. The posts are cleaned with `clean_texts`. A `TfidfVectorizer` of its own (`max_features=1000`), separate from chapter 6's, learns on the whole table (no labels, so no test set to leak into); it leaves out the negation words kept by chapter 3, "face" (the generic word of emoji names) and, with `"btc"`, "bitcoin" and "btc", because they would sit in every topic name. `KMeans(n_clusters=N_TOPICS, n_init=10, random_state=RANDOM_SEED)` groups the TF-IDF vectors; it runs in a single thread (`threadpoolctl`), because KMeans adds up its sums in several threads in a varying order, which can change the last digits and in rare cases a topic. A topic's name is the five words with the largest values in its centre; its quotes are the original texts of the three posts closest to the centre, a text already shown being skipped; only posts with at least one word of the vocabulary are quoted, unless a topic has no other posts (a post without any such word is a vector of zeros and can sit close to a centre near zero without sharing anything with the topic). With the same data source and switches, every run gives the same topics.

**Drawers.** Automatic topics leave one large catch-all topic, and word lists alone miss new subjects, so chapter 9 adds two layers after the topics (a third, single events found day by day, needs dated posts and is not part of this notebook). **Layer 1:** every post of `topic_posts` goes into the drawers of the word list whose words it contains: for each drawer, its excluded phrases are first replaced by a space in the original `text` (not `clean_text`, which breaks phrases apart), then one of its words or phrases found as a whole word (`\b…\b`), ignoring case, with a space in a phrase matching any whitespace, puts the post in. A post can go into several drawers or into none, so drawer sizes add up to more than the number of posts. Every drawer shows five posts drawn at random with `RANDOM_SEED` (different texts) with the words found in them, so that a person can see what it really catches. **Layer 2:** the posts in no drawer are grouped with `topic_vectors`, `group_vectors` and `describe_groups` (learned on these posts only, `N_OTHER_GROUPS` groups); the groups are only a hint, and the program never adds a drawer or changes a word list. The word list holds general words only, no names of single events, projects, companies, exchanges, people or countries (stock market indices and index funds such as nasdaq or spy, and government bodies such as sec or fed, count as general words); coin names appear only in the drawer `other_coins`. **Version and fingerprint:** a word list has a version, which goes up whenever a word changes; its fingerprint is the first 16 hexadecimal digits of the SHA-256 of `json.dumps([[drawer, name, words, exclude], ...], ensure_ascii=True, separators=(",", ":"))` over the drawers in order (the version itself is not part of it). The checks pin the fingerprint of every released built-in version (`DRAWER_FINGERPRINTS` in `scripts/check.py`), so a word of the built-in list cannot change without a new version; a new version adds a line there. Chapter 10 gives every drawer the same row as a topic (`why_row`: bullish share by label, at 0.5 and calibrated, the fair comparison, both too-few marks, the difference from all), plus the rows `any_drawer` and `no_drawer`, which add up to the overall row. On screen the drawers are sorted by bullish share by label; in the brief they keep the word-list order, so that runs can be compared drawer by drawer. The topics and the topic × bullish table stay as they are, as the comparison.

**Why.** Chapter 10 scores the `clean_text` of every post in `topic_posts`, neutral posts included, with `sentiment_proba` (with two classes the model never learned neutral posts, so scoring them uses the model beyond what it was trained for; the notebook says so). The probability of bullish becomes a call with `BULLISH_THRESHOLD` and `BEARISH_THRESHOLD`. For the whole topic post table and for every topic it reports the posts per label and the **bullish share by label** (bullish ÷ (bullish + bearish), neutral left out) with its difference from the whole table in percentage points; the model's calls, the **bullish share by model** (bullish ÷ (bullish + bearish) calls, unsure left out) and the unsure posts as a share of all posts; and a **fair comparison** on the posts the model never saw (`post_id` not in `train_post_ids`) that are labelled bearish or bullish: both bullish shares on those posts and the model minus the labels in percentage points. Posts whose text equals a training post but whose id differs count as unseen; how many are in the comparison is recorded. A share with a zero denominator is `null` in the brief and "—" in the table, and so is every difference computed from it. Topics are sorted by bullish share by label, highest first, `null` last, ties by topic number. A topic with fewer than `MIN_TOPIC_POSTS` posts is marked: the default 200 is a rule of thumb (if about half of a topic's posts are neutral, 200 posts leave about 100 bullish or bearish posts, and with 100 posts a share near one half can be off by about 10 percentage points by chance), not a statistical test.

**Calibration.** Counting the model's calls gives a biased share, because the model finds bullish and bearish posts differently well: if a share *t* of the bullish posts and a share *f* of the bearish posts are called bullish, a group with a true bullish share *p* shows *h* = *p* × *t* + (1 − *p*) × *f* on average. Chapter 10 therefore also splits every post at 0.5 (`half_call`), counts the **share at 0.5** *h*, and **calibrates** it: (*h* − *f*) ÷ (*t* − *f*), clipped to [0, 1]. *t* is the bullish recall and *f* is 1 − the bearish recall of the chosen model in chapter 8's evaluation table, on the test posts that match the topic posts (`test_mentions_btc` with `TOPIC_SCOPE = "btc"`, `all_test` with `"all"`); nothing is trained or evaluated again. With two classes the 0.5 split gives exactly chapter 8's labels, which the notebook checks on the test posts (with three classes, on the test posts chapter 8 called bearish or bullish); it differs from `model_call` only when both thresholds are 0.5 and a probability is exactly 0.5 (bearish here, bullish there). The calibrated share is `null` when the share at 0.5 is `null`, when a rate is `null` (`rates_missing`), when *t* − *f* is below `MIN_RATE_GAP` or not above 0 (`rate_gap_too_small`), and with three classes (`three_classes`: 1 − the bearish recall then also counts bearish posts called neutral). The calibrated share is the model's bullish share to use; the columns with the 0.3 / 0.7 thresholds stay as a comparison. Every row, the overall row included, gets the calls at 0.5, both shares and `too_few_bullish_bearish` (fewer bearish + bullish posts by label than `MIN_BULLISH_BEARISH_POSTS`; it changes no number); every fair comparison gets the same shares and their differences from the labels. A whole row's shares at 0.5 cover neutral posts too, so they are compared with the labels only in the fair comparison. The rates come from test posts, and the test posts in the topic post table are also in the fair comparison, so the calibrated share there is a little optimistic; their number is recorded (`n_rate_posts_in_fair_comparison`).

**Emotions.** Chapter 11 learns eight crypto emotions and `none` from the pre-labels of [Emotion pre-labels](#emotion-pre-labels), read from `EMOTION_LABELS_SOURCE`. These are **silver labels**: a language model gave them, not a person. The networks learn from them and are tested on them, so the evaluation shows how well the networks learned to give the silver labels, not how well they recognise emotions, and a high score does not show that emotions are recognised correctly; how often the silver labels are right can only be measured with the review table. **Reading.** The file must have exactly the five columns, every id once, every emotion one of the nine labels or `unparsed`, every id a post of `df_all` and every fingerprint that of the post's text; otherwise the notebook stops and says that the label file and the data source are not the same set (the sample and the full dataset number their posts separately). A file that cannot be read stops the notebook with its path, a note that the default address can be read only after the file has been pushed to GitHub, and the local copy to use instead. **Which posts.** Four steps leave posts out, in this order, and the brief records each step's posts and labels: `unparsed`; `none` when `EMOTION_KEEP_NONE` is `False`; emotions not in `EMOTION_NAMES`; emotions with fewer than `EMOTION_MIN_CLASS_POSTS` posts left. What is left is the emotion data; its classes follow `EMOTION_NAMES` with `none` last, and fewer than two classes stop the notebook. **The chain** is that of chapters 3 to 8: `clean_texts`; an 80/20 split and a half-and-half split of the 20%, both stratified by emotion with `RANDOM_SEED`; a `CountVectorizer` and a `TfidfVectorizer` (`max_features=VOCAB_SIZE`, 32-bit floats, sparse) fitted on the emotion training part only; one Keras network per vector type, a single `Dense` layer with one softmax unit per class, sparse categorical cross-entropy, Adam, `EPOCHS` and `BATCH_SIZE`, after `tf.keras.utils.set_random_seed(RANDOM_SEED)`; the network with the higher macro-F1 on the validation part is chosen (a tie goes to `count_network`); the test part is evaluated with the measures and baselines of chapter 8 (always majority, scikit-learn taking the first class in alphabetical order on a tie; uniform random with `RANDOM_SEED`), and every network's macro-F1 must beat both baselines. **Emotion calls.** The chosen network gives every post of `topic_posts` the emotion with the highest probability (`emotion_posts`). The brief gives the posts per emotion and their shares for all topic posts, for every drawer, for the posts in at least one drawer and for those in no drawer (these two add up to all topic posts); the counts include posts of the emotion data, so they describe the posts and do not evaluate anything. Example posts: at most 3 per emotion for all topic posts and 1 per emotion for every other group, only from posts that are not in the emotion data, highest probability first, every text once per group; on the sample, where every topic post has a pre-label, there may be none. **Review.** `EMOTION_REVIEW_SOURCE` can name a filled-in review table; `emotion_review_agreement` then gives the rows filled in and the unweighted agreement, overall and per pre-label (an overall agreement needs the weighting described in [The review table](#the-review-table)).

**Dated data.** Chapter 12 reads two public datasets with dates, both by Hernandez Cruz et al. under CC BY-NC 4.0: the tweets of `ExponentialScience/DLT-Tweets` (about 22 million English tweets about blockchains and crypto, mostly from 2021 to mid-2023, without ids or authors) as the `retail` layer, and the news of `ExponentialScience/DLT-Sentiment-News` (23,301 CryptoPanic headlines with summaries and reader votes, January 2021 to May 2025) as the `media` layer; `kol` is an allowed layer without data so far. Both become one **dated post table** (`dated_posts`, shape in [docs/dated-posts.md](docs/dated-posts.md)); every daily result is counted by its `date`, the UTC day. **Steps**, in this order, with the posts left after each recorded in the brief: `read`; `time_parsed` (`parse_times`: a time with a time zone is converted to UTC, one without is taken as UTC, a date alone gives the day and no time, anything else cannot be parsed and is left out; the tweets write their times in four ways, the news in one); `in_dates` (`DATED_START` to `DATED_END`); `language` (`DATED_LANGUAGE`; the news have no language column and the dataset card says they are all English); `mentions_btc` (with `DATED_BTC_ONLY`, chapter 1's rule on the text after the replacements); `unique` (one row per `post_id`); `daily_cap` (at most `DATED_DAILY_CAP` posts per layer and UTC day). **Replacements.** Before the id is made and the Bitcoin rule looks at it, every text gets two replacements: a link (text starting with `http://`, `https://` or `www.`, in any case, up to the next whitespace) becomes `http`, and an account name (`@` and letters, digits or underscores, where the character before `@` is not one of these) becomes `@user`; an e-mail address therefore stays. Example posts then show no other people's account names, and account names and links do not become one-off "new words" in later counts per day. **Ids.** A tweet's id is `dlt-tweets:` and the first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of its original time text and its replaced text; a news item's id is `dlt-news:` and the same from its original time text and its CryptoPanic address (so that `NEWS_TEXT` does not change it). Rows with the same id are the same post written twice; one is kept, in a fixed order, and the brief counts the others. **The cap** keeps, per layer and day, the posts with the smallest draw keys (`"<RANDOM_SEED>:<post_id>"` hashed), which is a random draw fixed by the seed and does not depend on reading order: the tweets are read one file at a time, in batches of three columns, and after each file only the posts still among the smallest keys of their day are kept. **News labels.** The dataset's `market_direction` was made from the reader votes (news with at least the median number of votes; below the 25th percentile of a vote share bearish, above the 75th bullish, neutral between). Its coding is 0 neutral, 1 bearish, 2 bullish, unlike this project's, so it is converted one by one: 0 → 1, 1 → 0, 2 → 2; the file's class names are checked to be neutral, bearish, bullish in this order. With `NEWS_LABEL = "votes"` the label is the side with more votes instead. The tweet dataset's own sentiment columns come from someone else's model and are not read. The news source is the domain of the original article (`unknown` when there is none); the tweets' source is `twitter`. The chapter only reads and counts; it cleans and scores nothing. The `dated_data` section of the brief carries its own period: the table's first and last day, and each layer's (the tweets end in mid-2023, the news in 2025).

**Day by day.** Chapter 13 cleans every post of `dated_posts` with `clean_texts` (every different text once; the cleaned texts are cached), scores the cleaned text with `sentiment_proba` (the probability of bullish as in chapter 10, split at 0.5 with `half_call`), gives it the emotion with the highest probability of `emotion_proba`, and puts its text into drawers with `drawer_matches`: `dated_scores`. The two recognition rates for the calibration are read again from chapter 8's evaluation table, the row of the chosen model on `test_mentions_btc` when `DATED_BTC_ONLY` is true and on `all_test` otherwise (when that is the row chapter 10 uses, the two sets of rates must be identical, which the notebook checks); the status follows chapter 10's rules, and unless it is `applied` every calibrated share of the chapter is `null`. The rates were measured on StockTwits test posts, so using them for tweets and news headlines is an assumption. A **pile** is all posts of one layer on one UTC day (or over its whole period), or those of them in one drawer: its posts, its calls at 0.5, the **counted bullish share** (bullish ÷ posts) and the **calibrated bullish share** (`calibrate_bullish_share`), both `null` and the pile marked `too_few_posts` when it has fewer than `DAILY_MIN_POSTS` posts (a day without posts too). Every layer gets a row for its whole period and one for every calendar day of its period, the days of `dated_data`, each with its pile, its posts per emotion (the classes the emotion network learned, `emotion_classes`: an emotion left out of training cannot be given), its posts in any drawer and in none, and one pile per drawer of the word list. Only the drawers of the word list are used: the groups of the posts in no drawer are found afresh in every run from other posts, so they cannot be compared from day to day. The whole period's counts are the sums of the days'. For every day of `EVENT_DAYS` and every layer, the `daily` section also holds up to `EVENT_EXAMPLES` posts with a probability of bullish at or above `BULLISH_THRESHOLD` (highest first) and as many at or below `BEARISH_THRESHOLD` (lowest first), every text once, and that day's new words; a day outside a layer's period is marked `in_period: false` there. These are the only uses of the two thresholds in the chapter. The sentiment model learned from StockTwits posts and the emotion network from silver labels on StockTwits posts: on tweets and news headlines their numbers are a reference, not a measurement.

**New words.** For every layer and day of chapter 13, a word is a piece of the cleaned text between spaces, counted once per post; its count on a day is the layer's posts that day containing it, and its past daily mean the counts of the `NEW_WORD_WINDOW` calendar days before, added up and divided by `NEW_WORD_WINDOW` (a day without posts counts 0). A **new word** has a count of at least `NEW_WORD_MIN_COUNT` and at least `NEW_WORD_RATIO` times its past daily mean; with a past daily mean of 0 only the first condition applies and its ratio is `null`. The first `NEW_WORD_WINDOW` days of a layer are not judged. Never new: the words of `NEW_WORD_IGNORED` (NLTK's English stop words, which include the negations that cleaning keeps, and `never`; `bitcoin` and `btc`; `user` and `http`, which account names and links became in chapter 12), words without a letter and words of a single character. A day reports at most `NEW_WORD_MAX_PER_DAY` new words: most posts first, then the higher ratio (`null` highest), then the word in alphabetical order. Every new word has up to `NEW_WORD_QUOTES` (3) example posts of that layer and day whose cleaned text contains it, in `post_id` order, every text once. No model is involved, so the same switches always give the same new words; the program names no events. The defaults of the three rules were fixed before any new word was looked at (see [Switches](#switches)).

**Two layers.** Chapter 14 compares the media layer (news headlines) and the retail layer (tweets) on the days that both cover: from the later of the two first days to the earlier of the two last days (`null` and no days when a layer is missing or the periods do not meet). It uses chapter 13's calibrated bullish share of every day as it is; a day marked too few there, or without a calibrated share, takes no part. On a day that takes part, a layer **leans bullish** when its share is at least 0.5 + `LAYER_GAP`, **bearish** when it is at most 0.5 − `LAYER_GAP`; the two layers **lean opposite ways** when one leans bullish and the other bearish (`null` when a layer takes no part). 0.5 is the middle, not each layer's own average: simple and easy to explain, but headlines and tweets are written in a different tone, so a layer that is always a little more bullish can make days look opposite partly because of its tone. With the default switches the media layer has a few headlines a day, so with `DAILY_MIN_POSTS = 100` almost none of its days takes part. The chapter also measures the sentiment model on the news headlines with a bullish or bearish reader label, over the media layer's whole period: the **true bullish rate** (labelled bullish and called bullish at 0.5 ÷ labelled bullish) and the **false bullish rate** (labelled bearish but called bullish ÷ labelled bearish), next to the two rates measured on StockTwits test posts, and three bullish shares of the same headlines: by the readers' labels, counted at 0.5, and calibrated with the StockTwits rates. Neutral news take no part; no news item was ever used for training. It describes news headlines and the readers' votes, not comments, and the news rates calibrate nothing. All of it goes into the `layers` section.

**Against the market.** Chapter 15 needs every layer's period, from the earliest first day to the latest last day, plus one day for the price. The **BTC daily closes**: with `PRICE_SOURCE = "auto"` it tries, in this order, Yahoo Finance through `yfinance` (`BTC-USD`; a daily bar runs from 00:00 to 24:00 UTC), Coinbase's public daily candles (`BTC-USD`, at most 300 days per request, fetched page by page; a candle runs from 00:00 to 24:00 UTC) and Stooq's daily CSV file (`BTCUSD`; Stooq does not say where its day ends), and uses the first that has every needed day. A source that cannot be reached, answers with an error or a web page, asks for a key or an account, or misses a needed day is recorded with its reason and the next one is tried; when none works, the prices are "not obtained", their correlations are `null` and the notebook goes on. `yfinance` would accept a Yahoo cookie consent page on its own; the chapter switches that off (tested with the pinned `yfinance` 1.7.0), so such an answer counts as not obtained. Every request has a timeout of `WEB_TIMEOUT` (30) seconds and is never repeated. The **Fear and Greed Index** comes from alternative.me's public address, one value from 0 to 100 per day, stamped at 00:00 UTC of its day (data from [alternative.me](https://alternative.me/crypto/fear-and-greed-index/), which asks to be named next to its data; the chapter does so wherever it shows the index). Its makers combine volatility (25%), momentum and volume (25%), social media posts on X (15%), surveys (15%, paused), Bitcoin's dominance (10%) and search trends (10%). For every layer, on its own days with a calibrated share, the chapter pairs day d with the index of day d and with the **next day's change**, close of day d + 1 ÷ close of day d − 1, and reports the number of days and **Spearman's rank correlation** (equal values at their average rank, as in pandas and SciPy): `null` with fewer than `VALIDATION_MIN_DAYS` days or a single value on either side. No p-value, no test, no forecast, no back test and no advice to buy or sell. Two limits: research has found only weak evidence that the mood of the crowd predicts prices; and 15% of the index comes from social media statistics, partly the same kind of source as the shares here, so the correlation with the index comes out higher than with an independent measure. The brief's `validation` section records where the closes and the index come from, the days needed and covered, and the coefficients; it stores no daily price or index value. `PRICE_SOURCE` and `FEAR_GREED_SOURCE` may name local files instead (the quick check does), or be `"none"`.

**Cache.** Chapter 1 stores `df_all`'s three loaded columns as Parquet in `{WORK_DIR}/cache/posts-<fingerprint>.parquet`. The fingerprint of a local file is a SHA-256 hash of its bytes (any edit forces a fresh read); for a web address it is a hash of the address. Chapter 12 stores the tweets left after its steps and cap as Parquet in `{WORK_DIR}/cache/dated-tweets-<fingerprint>.parquet`, with the counts of every step in the file's metadata; the fingerprint covers the source (a local file or folder by the names and bytes of its `.parquet` files, a web address by the address), `DATED_START`, `DATED_END`, `DATED_LANGUAGE`, `DATED_BTC_ONLY`, `DATED_DAILY_CAP`, `RANDOM_SEED` and the table's shape version. The news (12 MB) are not cached. Chapter 13 stores the cleaned texts of the dated posts as Parquet in `{WORK_DIR}/cache/dated-clean-<fingerprint>.parquet`; the fingerprint covers `EMOJI_TO_WORDS`, `KEEP_NEGATIONS`, the cleaning steps, the versions of nltk, emoji, contractions and beautifulsoup4, both dated sources and every post's id and text, so a changed post or switch never reads an old result. Chapter 15 stores what it fetched from the web as Parquet in `{WORK_DIR}/cache/price-btc-usd-<source>.parquet` and `{WORK_DIR}/cache/fear-greed.parquet`, with the source, the UTC day of the fetch and the days it was needed for in the file's metadata; it uses a cache only when it covers every day the run needs (for the index, from the same address), and otherwise fetches again and replaces it, so a change of the date switches never leaves days out. `yfinance` keeps its cookie and time zone files in `{WORK_DIR}/cache/yfinance/`, not in the home folder.

### The brief

The brief has a fixed format, version **1.6**. [docs/brief-format.md](docs/brief-format.md) describes it field by field (type, whether the field is always there, whether it may be `null`, what it means), says which posts each section covers, where daily results will go and when the version changes; [docs/brief.schema.json](docs/brief.schema.json) holds the same rules as a JSON Schema. In short:

- Top level, always present: `format_version` (the version above), `generated_at` (UTC, ISO 8601), `scope`, `dataset` (chapters 0 to 3), `sentiment` (chapters 4 to 8), `topics` (chapter 9), `drawers` (chapter 9), `why` (chapter 10), `emotions` (chapter 11), `dated_data` (chapter 12), `daily` and `new_words` (chapter 13), `layers` (chapter 14) and `validation` (chapter 15). Every section starts as `null` in the notebook and is filled by its chapter before the brief is saved.
- `scope`: `source` (the `DATA_SOURCE` value), `granularity` (`"batch"`: one result for the whole dataset; results per day sit in sections of their own with their own period, so far `dated_data`, `daily`, `new_words`, `layers` and `validation`; `"daily"` is reserved for a brief whose every section is per day and not allowed in the current format), `coins` (`"all"` or `"btc_only"`, describing the working sample only), `n_classes`. Each section's own fields say which posts it uses: with the default switches the models learn from posts about all coins, while the topics and the topic × bullish table use only posts that mention Bitcoin.
- A value that cannot be computed (for example a share with a zero denominator) is `null`, never 0, NaN or infinity, and only the fields the format document marks "Null: yes" may be `null`. `save_brief` converts numpy and pandas values to plain values (NaN and infinity become `null`), checks the result against the rules, and writes it only when it passes; the JSON writer also refuses NaN.
- The rules allow no other fields. Adding or changing a field means changing the rules, the format document and the format version together (see [Versions](docs/brief-format.md#versions) and [Editing the notebook](#editing-the-notebook)).

## Editing the notebook

1. Edit `notebook/ledger_eyes.py` only.
2. Regenerate the notebook: `scripts/build_notebook.sh` (writes `notebook/ledger_eyes.ipynb`; cell ids come from the cell content, so an unchanged source gives a byte-identical file).
3. Run `scripts/check.sh quick`.

Changing the brief's format (a new field, a changed field): change `docs/brief.schema.json`; copy its text unchanged into `BRIEF_SCHEMA_TEXT` in chapter 16; set the new `format_version` in the rules' `const`, in the cell that creates `brief` and in `docs/brief-format.md`, following [Versions](docs/brief-format.md#versions); update the field tables of `docs/brief-format.md`. The locked counterexamples in `scripts/check.py` may only be added to.

Writing rules for the notebook: one cell does one thing; every code cell, or short run of related code cells, is preceded by an explanation cell; explanation cells are English, then a line with `***`, then Chinese; code comments are English only. New switches go into the parameters cell in the same form, all package installs stay in the single install cell right after it, and chapter 16 (format, check, save and panel) stays the last chapter: a new chapter goes before it.

### Text rules

The quick check scans every text file in the repository outside `data/` and `.venv/` and rejects:

- private numbering, in the forms the private log uses: file-name prefixes (two digits, optionally one of the capital letters A–D or Q, optionally a round suffix `-R` or `-` plus a number, then `_W` and a week number), review codes (two digits, a capital A–D, then `-R`), `W` followed by two digits, `Q` followed by two digits, and week numbers written in Chinese. A bare "two digits and a letter" is deliberately not flagged, because it collides with normal notation such as 50D (50 days) or 10B (10 billion). Before scanning, the check tests these rules on built-in examples (`NUMBERING_MUST_MATCH` and `NUMBERING_MUST_NOT_MATCH` in `scripts/check.py`): every log code form must be caught and notation such as 50D, 30D, 7D, 10B or W2 must not be; if either fails, the check fails;
- names of chat assistants and their vendors, the two-letter capitalised abbreviation for artificial intelligence, and co-author trailers;
- local home paths and user names.

Chinese text is allowed only in the notebook's explanation cells, after the `***` line. The exact patterns are in `FORBIDDEN_PATTERNS` in `scripts/check.py`.

## Emotion pre-labels

Chapter 11 learns crypto emotions from posts, and no public dataset labels them, so a local language model labels StockTwits posts first: `scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh` asks the model `qwen2.5:7b`, through an [Ollama](https://ollama.com) server running on this machine, to give every post one of nine labels. These are **silver labels**: a 7-billion-parameter model gave them, not a person, and they are not guaranteed to be right. The [review table](#the-review-table) lets a person check a part of them and measure how often the model agrees.

### The nine labels

Eight crypto emotions and `none`, in this order (the order of every table and of the review draw):

| Label | Meaning (as given to the model) |
| --- | --- |
| `panic` | afraid of losing money, rushing to sell, shouting that the price will crash |
| `greed` | wanting to get rich fast, fear of missing out, going all in, gambling with leverage |
| `excitement` | thrilled by a price rise or good news, cheering the price on |
| `anger` | angry at someone or something, such as an exchange, regulators, whales or other posters |
| `mockery` | sarcasm or ridicule, laughing at others for losing money or for believing the wrong thing |
| `despair` | hopeless after a loss, giving up, feeling that it is all over |
| `wait_and_see` | undecided or cautious, waiting for confirmation before acting |
| `confidence` | calm certainty, holding for the long term, sure of one's own judgement |
| `none` | no emotion: news, a neutral fact, a plain question, or only a link |

A tenth value, `unparsed`, only means that the model gave no usable answer in the allowed attempts; it is not an emotion.

### Requirements

- [Ollama](https://ollama.com) installed and running on this machine (`ollama serve`, listening on `http://127.0.0.1:11434`), with the model already installed: `ollama pull qwen2.5:7b` (about 4.7 GB). The script never downloads a model; when the model is missing it stops before labelling anything.
- The environment of [Run locally](#run-locally), and for the full dataset `scripts/download_data.sh`.

The script talks only to the Ollama server, and only on a loopback address (`127.0.0.1`, `::1` or `localhost` over `http://`); any other address is refused before anything else happens. It connects with Python's standard library, ignores every proxy setting (a request to `127.0.0.1` must not pass through a proxy program), and needs no key or account. Neither check contacts Ollama.

### Running

```bash
scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh label --sample          # every post of the sample -> samples/emotions-prelabel-sample.csv
scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh label                   # 4,000 posts of the full dataset -> labels/emotions-prelabel-v1.csv
scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh label --retry-unparsed  # ask again only for the rows written as unparsed
scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh review-template         # labels/emotions-review-template.csv
scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh export-review           # the review rows with their texts -> data/review/emotions-review-with-text.csv
scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh show-prompt             # the fixed prompt, the answer format and their fingerprint
```

A normal answer takes about half a second on a recent laptop, so the sample takes a few minutes and the default 4,000 posts well under an hour; the full run can be left running in the background and resumed at any time (see below).

**Which posts.** The posts are read with the same code and ids as chapter 1. Posts whose text is empty after stripping spaces (those counted by `n_empty_text`) are left out. With a count, round(count × Bitcoin share) posts are drawn from the posts that mention Bitcoin (the `mentions_btc` rule) and the rest from the other posts, each with `DataFrame.sample(random_state=seed)`; a pool that is too small is taken whole, and the run prints how many posts it drew from each pool. The drawn posts are then shuffled with the same seed, and that is the processing order: a run stopped half-way has labelled a random part with about the same share of Bitcoin posts. The default share 0.75 favours Bitcoin posts, which the topic chapters look at, and keeps a quarter from other coins so that a model trained on the labels has seen those too. `--count all` takes every post (the sample uses it).

**Asking the model.** One request per post to Ollama's `/api/chat`: a system message (what the posts are, the nine labels with their meanings, the rules), ten hand-written example posts with their answers (none of them is a post of the dataset), then the post itself. The dataset's own bullish/bearish label is never read or sent, so that the emotions stay separate from the sentiment of chapters 4 to 8. The request uses temperature 0, the seed as the model's seed, no streaming, at most 64 tokens per answer (the longest answer, `{"emotion": "wait_and_see"}`, needs about 10), and Ollama's structured output with a JSON Schema that allows only `{"emotion": <one of the labels>}`. The answer is read as JSON; its `emotion`, stripped and in lower case, must be one of the labels.

**When something goes wrong.**

- An answer that cannot be read, or a request that takes longer than the timeout: the post is asked again with a fixed reminder added to the system message (asked the same way at temperature 0, the model would give the same answer). After 3 attempts the post is written as `unparsed` and the run goes on with the next post.
- The server cannot be reached (connection refused or reset, or a server error 5xx): the script waits and tries again; when that fails too, the whole run stops and the post is not written at all, so that an interruption never turns the remaining posts into `unparsed`. Start the server and run the same command again.
- Any other error answer of the server (for example a model that is not installed) stops the run.

**Resuming.** Every labelled post is appended as one line and flushed to disk at once. A new run reads the file and skips every id already in it, `unparsed` ones included. A partial last line, left by a run that was stopped while writing, is dropped and that post labelled again. `--retry-unparsed` asks again only for the `unparsed` rows and replaces each row in place (the file is rewritten through a temporary file, so its order and row count stay the same). A file whose rows come from another model, another prompt version or another data source (an id missing from the source, or a text fingerprint that differs) stops the run instead of being added to. `--limit N` caps the posts asked in one run. Every run adds one line to `data/prelabel/runs.jsonl`: start and end, posts labelled, seconds, attempts, whether and why it stopped (no text). Progress goes to the terminal; redirect it into `data/` when the run is left in the background.

That is why there are two label files, which must never be mixed, and why every row carries its text fingerprint: a chapter that joins labels to posts can compare the fingerprints and refuse a label file from the other source.

**Where the files come from.** The post ids and fingerprints refer to the StockTwits crypto dataset ([ElKulako/stocktwits-crypto](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ElKulako/stocktwits-crypto), MIT License). The labels were given on a local machine by `qwen2.5:7b` through Ollama with prompt v1: silver labels from a model, not human annotation.

### Prompt versions

The prompt lives in the script: `PROMPT_VERSION = "v1"`, the labels and their meanings (`LABELS_V1`), the hand-written examples (`EXAMPLES_V1`), the system message, the retry reminder and the answer format. Its fingerprint is the SHA-256 of `json.dumps({"first": <messages of a first request>, "retry": <messages of a retry>, "format": <answer format>}, ensure_ascii=True, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"))`, with the placeholder `<post>` in place of the post; `show-prompt` prints the whole prompt and the fingerprint, and the quick check pins it for every released version (`PROMPT_FINGERPRINTS` in `scripts/check.py`). Once a file labelled with a version is committed, the text of that version never changes: a changed character, or another label list, needs a new version, which also gives new default file names (`labels/emotions-prelabel-v2.csv` and so on) and a new line in `PROMPT_FINGERPRINTS`. A label file never mixes models or prompt versions; the script stops instead.

Another label list goes into a JSON file passed with `--labels-file`: `{"prompt_version": "v2", "labels": [{"name": "...", "meaning": "..."}, ...], "examples": [{"post": "...", "emotion": "..."}, ...]}`. Names are lower-case letters and `_`; the list must contain `none`; `unparsed` is reserved; and `v1` is refused, because it is the built-in prompt.

### The review table

`labels/emotions-review-template.csv` holds 200 rows for a person to check, with three columns: `post_id`, `prelabel` (the model's label) and `human_label` (empty). The rows are drawn from `labels/emotions-prelabel-v1.csv`, leaving out `unparsed`, **taking the labels in turn**: the rows of every label (in file order) are shuffled with their own `random.Random(42)`; then, round after round, every label in the order of the label table gives its next row, a label with no rows left being skipped, until 200 rows are drawn or none are left. The rows of the table are in drawing order. With enough rows in every label this gives 22 or 23 rows per label (23 for the first two); a rare label gives all its rows and the other labels fill its places. Taking the labels in turn makes it possible to measure, label by label, how often the model is right.

To fill it in, run `scripts/prelabel_emotions.sh export-review`: it writes the same rows, in the same order, with the original texts to `data/review/emotions-review-with-text.csv`, which Git ignores; **this file with the texts must never enter the repository.** Read a text there and write your label (one of the nine) into `human_label` of the template. A label's agreement is the share of its rows whose `human_label` equals `prelabel`. Because every label has about the same number of rows in the table while the labels are not equally common, **an overall agreement must weight each label's agreement by that label's share of the labelled (not `unparsed`) rows of `labels/emotions-prelabel-v1.csv`**; the plain share of matching rows in the table is not the overall agreement.

## Known limitations

- **59 posts lose their text.** In `st-data-full.xlsx`, 59 posts begin with `=`, so the spreadsheet stores them as formulas whose stored value is an error such as `#NAME?` (for example row 365284 of `stocktwits_1`, originally "=the new btc"). pandas, on Colab as well as locally, reads these cells as empty, so they appear in `df_all` as empty strings with their labels intact and are counted in `n_empty_text`. That is 59 of 1,331,697 posts; the notebook keeps them as they are rather than reading the file a second way. Five of them mention Bitcoin, so the Bitcoin count is 5 lower than it would be with the original text.
- The notebook has not been run on Colab itself yet, so neither has the panel: whether Gradio shows it inside Colab without a public link is untested.
- Drawers match words literally. A word with a second meaning can put a post into the wrong drawer when no excluded phrase catches it (for example "sec" in "back in 10 sec"); the spot checks are there to show such cases, and the remedy is a new version of the word list.
- The emotion pre-labels are silver labels from a 7-billion-parameter model, not human annotation; how often they are right is unknown until the review table is filled in. Temperature 0 and a fixed seed usually give the same label for the same post again, but Ollama does not promise it across versions and machines.
- Chapter 11's networks learn from silver labels and are evaluated on silver labels of the same kind: their scores show how well they learned to give those labels, not how well they recognise emotions, until a person has filled in the review table.
- Chapter 11's default `EMOTION_LABELS_SOURCE` is this repository's pre-label file on GitHub. It can be read only once the file has been pushed there (and with a network connection); that address has not been tried yet. Until then, set the switch to the local `labels/emotions-prelabel-v1.csv`.
- Chapter 12 reads the tweets straight from Hugging Face by default (3.6 GB in 14 files); this has not been tried on Colab, and a free Colab session needs a while for it. The local checks read the downloaded copies.
- Many tweets give only a date, without a time (`time` stays empty for them), and the dataset does not say in which time zone its times without a zone were written; they are taken as UTC.
- Two tweets with the same time text and the same text after the replacements count as one post (`unique`); with date-only times this also merges identical texts posted by different accounts on the same day.
- The tweets have no authors, so the `kol` layer has no data yet.
- Chapter 13 borrows models that learned from StockTwits posts (the emotions from silver labels): their bullish shares and emotions on tweets and news headlines are a reference, not a measurement, and the recognition rates that calibrate the shares were measured on StockTwits test posts, not on these texts.
- The media layer has a few headlines a day (with the default switches a median of 2), so with `DAILY_MIN_POSTS = 100` almost none of its days has a bullish share, and with `NEW_WORD_MIN_COUNT = 10` it rarely, if ever, has a new word; its whole period has a share.
- A new word is a word, not an event: one post repeated by many accounts or a campaign of near-identical posts can make words new as well.
- With the full data the brief is much larger than before (more than ten megabytes), mostly the `daily` rows of every layer and day and the example posts of the new words.
- Chapter 15 describes the past and predicts nothing. Research has found only weak evidence that the mood of the crowd predicts prices, and 15% of the Fear and Greed Index comes from social media statistics, partly the same kind of source as the shares here, so a correlation with the index comes out higher than with an independent measure.
- The media layer has a few headlines a day, so with the default `DAILY_MIN_POSTS` almost none of its days has a calibrated share: chapter 14 then has few or no days on which both layers take part, and the media layer's correlations in chapter 15 are `null`.
- Chapter 14 measures the sentiment model on news headlines with a label made from reader votes (the middle half of the vote share is neutral and takes no part); it says nothing about how well the model reads comments or tweets.
- Stooq has answered recent requests with a web page that checks the browser with JavaScript instead of its CSV file; chapter 15 counts that as not obtained and never tries to get past such a check.
- Chapter 15 switches off the cookie consent that `yfinance` would otherwise accept on Yahoo's consent page; this was tested with the pinned `yfinance` 1.7.0, and a Colab session with another preinstalled version of `yfinance` may differ. The price sources and alternative.me have not been tried from Colab.
- The Fear and Greed Index misses a few days of its history; such a day takes no part.
- Training is seeded, but TensorFlow does not promise bit-identical results across runs and machines, so model scores (and, when the two are close, the chosen model) may differ slightly. Counts and the two baseline rows do not depend on training.

## Data policy

The full dataset and everything computed from it (caches, executed notebooks, briefs, tables and quotes produced from the full data) stay in `data/`, which Git ignores. They are never committed, also not in this README or in `docs/`. Only the sample above and examples produced from the sample (or written by hand) may be committed; the examples in `docs/brief-format.md` are written by hand.

One exception: the three files of [Emotion pre-labels](#emotion-pre-labels) (`labels/emotions-prelabel-v1.csv`, `labels/emotions-review-template.csv` and `samples/emotions-prelabel-sample.csv`) are computed from the dataset, but they hold only post ids, labels, the model name, the prompt version and a short text fingerprint, not a single character of any post, so they are committed. Everything else computed from the full dataset stays out, including the review rows with their texts (`data/review/`).

The same holds for the two dated datasets of chapter 12: the downloaded copies (`data/raw/dlt-tweets/`, `data/raw/dlt-news/`), the tweet cache and every table and brief computed from them stay in `data/`. Only the two dated samples (CC BY-NC 4.0, see [The dated samples](#the-dated-samples)) and what is computed from them are committed.

The BTC daily closes and the Fear and Greed Index that chapter 15 reads from the web, its caches, and the layer comparisons and correlations computed from the full data stay in `data/` as well; the brief stores no daily price or index value. The only price file committed is the made-up one of [The validation samples](#the-validation-samples), and the only index data committed is the small part of alternative.me's index there, credited to alternative.me.
